# Research and discovery

**Original desk research: 7 November 2026. Publication review: 13 November 2026.**

The purpose was to understand the service before proposing software: what is publicly promised, what reviewers describe, and which questions deserve validation. No customer or cleaner interviews, internal operational data or authenticated access to the company’s applications were available. This is independent desk research, not an assessment commissioned by the company.

## Four levels of evidence

| Level | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Observed evidence** | A dated public statement or listing that can be inspected. Company claims remain company claims. |
| **User-reported feedback** | An individual account in a review; not independently verified and not representative of all users. |
| **Product hypothesis** | An inference that could be tested, not a confirmed operational defect. |
| **Design response** | A proposed or implemented demo behavior intended to address that hypothesis. |

## Reviews: useful questions, weak population evidence

**Customer feedback.** The [Trustpilot profile](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/menagesimple.com) and historical research notes were used to explore trust, continuity, communication and the handling of a concern. Accessible page versions and the original notes differed, so this showcase does not reproduce a current score, review count or complaint frequency. The available sample is incomplete and self-selected; no reviewer identities or quotations are reproduced.

**Cleaner and candidate feedback.** Available [Glassdoor reviews](https://www.glassdoor.fr/Avis/M%C3%A9nageSimple-Avis-E4567351.htm) mention practical topics such as scheduling, transport and material. A visible [Indeed review](https://ch-fr.indeed.com/cmp/M%C3%A9nagesimple) is positive about communication. The accessible samples are too small to characterize the workforce; allegations and personal details are deliberately excluded. Advertised terms are not evidence of employees’ actual experience.

**Google reviews.** The original notes refer to business-listing aggregates and indirect directory results. No complete, reproducible set of individual Google reviews was preserved. No Google score, volume or sentiment conclusion is used here.

## From evidence to decisions

| Evidence level | Hypothesis and potential impact | Design response | Validation still needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observed:** public equipment guidance makes the provider of some items relevant. | Equipment expectations may need explicit confirmation; uncertainty could interrupt a visit. | Show who supplies each relevant item and a preparation view. **Implemented demo.** | Observe preparation with real cleaners and equipment. |
| **User-reported:** feedback raises communication and continuity questions. | A request or change may need a shared record to avoid repeated explanations. | Preserve a structured mission across roles. **Implemented demo**, with full handovers **designed**. | Test whether the record contains the information each role actually needs. |
| **Observed:** recurring service and replacements appear in public positioning. | Continuity could depend on both assignment and transfer of instructions. | Version-aware briefs and independent replacement checks. Checks **implemented**; full replacement **designed/simulated**. | Run realistic change and substitution scenarios with users. |
| **User-reported:** practical constraints appear in a small employment-review sample. | An exception may require context and human judgment rather than automatic blame. | Incident context and human resolution. **Implemented demo**, with fairness of automated response rules still to review. | Assess escalation, workload and fairness with operational staff. |

These hypotheses do not assert fragmented systems or systemic failures at MénageSimple. They define a research agenda. The next step is to test them against actual workflows and existing tools before proposing integration.
